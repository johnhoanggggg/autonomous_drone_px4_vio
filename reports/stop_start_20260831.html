Three flights · 2026-08-31 06:41, 06:42, 06:48 UTC

Leave a path alone for 1.5 seconds and it flies at full speed.

It almost never gets 1.5 seconds. The stopping and starting is not a fault, a corner, or a yaw pause — it is the command limiter zeroing its speed every time a replacement path arrives, roughly once a second.

The measurement

Nothing is wrong when it stops

Across the three flights there are 27 discrete stops — commanded speed falling below 0.05 m/s. 24 of them begin while the adapter is in ROUTE and the follower is FOLLOWING and valid. No clearance event, no cross-track fault, no yaw alignment, no corner hold. The carrot is sitting at a median 0.250 m against a 0.25 m lookahead, so the follower is asking for exactly what it should.

20 of the 27 begin within 0.6 s of a path rebuild. That is the whole story, and the chart below is the proof.

The mechanism

A replacement path sets the command speed to zero

PathCommandLimiter::set_path has two ways to install a new route. If the geometry ahead of the command point is identical, the shared-suffix branch renumbers the arc length and keeps the speed. Otherwise it takes the join branch:

if (projection.cross_track > kEpsilon) {
    join_target_ = projection.point;
    join_limit_  = limit;
    speed_ = 0.0;            // command point restarts from rest
} else {
    position_ = projection.point;
    speed_ = std::min(max_speed_, std::hypot(velocity_.first, velocity_.second));
}

A replanned route is essentially never bit-identical through the standing command point, so kEpsilon is not a meaningful escape hatch — the join branch is the normal case. The command point then re-accelerates from zero at command_acceleration 0.30 m/s², needing 0.67 s to reach 0.20 m/s, and crawls through the bounded join band before it can advance along the path at all. That is the dip in Fig 1, and it lands squarely on top of the next rebuild.

The join itself is not gratuitous: it exists so an offset path is rejoined under the speed limit rather than snapped to. But zeroing the speed to do it is a much stronger measure than the geometry requires, and it is applied on every replan.

My fault

I doubled the rebuild rate last night

Fixing F3 I dropped path_retain_tolerance from 0.12 m to 0.04 m so a loop-closure correction would replan instead of faulting. It does. It also makes the planner replace the accepted route far more eagerly.

In fairness to the change

End-to-end goal closure did not get worse: 0.044 / 0.030 / 0.058 m/s before, 0.030 / 0.046 / 0.048 m/s after, and the fraction of maximum speed went up slightly (42–59% to 57–62%). The doubling did not cost measurable throughput. It is still the wrong direction, and it is the cheapest lever available — but it is not, on its own, why the drone is slow. The join is.

The other change I made

Raising the safety margin closed a neck

safety_margin 0.00 → 0.05 raises lethal_radius to 0.30 m. That bought real contact margin, and it cost 16–25% of the navigable area (4.50 → 3.78 m² on one map, 2.77 → 2.07 m² on the other). On 06:48 the pinch the vehicle needed measures 0.266–0.294 m — passable at the old 0.25 m requirement, not at the new one. Three CLEARANCE_BLOCKED events across the two later flights fired with the pose at 0.35–0.42 m, well clear: it was the chord to the lookahead that could not fit, not the vehicle.

This is a genuine trade and it is yours to make, not mine. 0.05 m of margin is the difference between "the airframe may reach 0.03 m inside an obstacle" and closing a doorway this environment actually has.

What to change

In this order

  1. Carry speed through the join. In the join branch, seed speed_ from the current command velocity instead of zero, clamped by what the join band and the clearance check already permit. This is the change that makes rebuild rate stop mattering, and it is the one that makes the drone fly through rather than stutter. It touches a safety-relevant limiter, so it wants its own tests and a props-off run.
  2. Then reconsider the cross-track budget as a set. With the join fixed, path_retain_tolerance can stay low without costing speed. Without it, raising retain back toward 0.06 buys a little and is capped by the retain < max_cross_track invariant I put in the launch guard — which is itself only tight because I set max_cross_track to 0.08.
  3. Decide the margin deliberately. Either accept 0.03 m of possible penetration and go back to a 0.25–0.28 m lethal radius so the necks in this space are flyable, or keep 0.30 m and accept that some doorways here are not autonomous routes. Both are defensible; drifting between them is not.
Working as intended

What last night's fixes did

The climb-phase grace fires and is visible in the bags: HOLD planner fault: follower validity is false; land countdown held while the route settles at 6.04 s and 11.04 s on the 06:41 flight, where the old build would have been six seconds from a landing. The 06:42 flight reached its goal. Minimum pose clearance across the three was 0.214–0.263 m with the required 0.30 m — tight, but no contact: peak lateral acceleration stayed inside the normal envelope on all three.

The cost is visible too. At max_cross_track 0.08 the 06:42 flight took six CROSS_TRACK_EXCEEDED events it would not have taken at 0.15. That was predicted and it is survivable now that a fault no longer runs the land clock during the settle — but it is the reason step 2 above is a set, not a single number.
